# Review: reading

Drafted by Sonnet (claude-sonnet), reviewed by Opus on 2026-11-20.

## Corrected
- The ρολόι, μπαλκόνι and χαλβάς examples had muddled read-as notes ("ό ι i", "sonra al-KO-ni"); they now give the whole word (ro-LO-i, bal-KO-ni, hal-VAS).
- The αυ/ευ rule listed Latin letters ("p, t, k, s, f…"); it now names the Greek voiceless consonants (θ, κ, ξ, π, σ, τ, φ, χ, ψ), which is the actual rule.
- The note on accented vowel pairs said "a pair" right after the consonant-pair section, so τσ could be read as the pair that splits. It now says vowel pairs (αι, ει, οι, ου), gives the normal case (είναι) and adds the diaeresis (Ζεϊνέπ), which the learner meets in the very next chapter.
- δύο and δέκα were glossed "DHI-o", "DHE-ka", which means nothing to a Turkish reader; now "THİ-o (δ: this'teki th)".
- Three checks had English-only options shown to every learner ("1st syllable (do)", "tsai (a and i read separately)", "u, as in Turkish su"); the options are now syllable spellings any reader can use (do-MA-ta, tsa-i, ya-UR-ti).
- Turkish suffixes glued to Greek (ρ'yi, πέντε'deki, τρία'dan) rephrased; "τσ ç'ye, τζ c'ye çok yakın" softened, since Greek τσ is [ts], not Turkish ç.

## Unsure
- μπ in the middle of a word is given as "mb"; many speakers say a plain b (μπαμπάς). Left as is: "mb" is the safe reading for a beginner.
- γ is described as a "soft ğ"; the Greek sound [ɣ] is stronger than most Turkish ğ. Acceptable with the ▶ audio.
