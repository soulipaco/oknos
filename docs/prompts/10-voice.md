# Phase 10 — A better Greek voice

Read `AGENTS.md`, `docs/SPEC.md` (§9, audio and speech) and `scripts/make_audio.py`. Decided with the maintainer on 2026-11-22: the app's spoken Greek is "just okay" and should become good, and the films in `docs/brand/animations/` should get a voice too.

## Where it stands (2026-11-22)

- **Words, sentences, dialogues:** the browser's own voice (Web Speech API). On Windows that is the operating system's old Greek voice (Microsoft Stefanos): robotic, sometimes wrong stress.
- **Listening passages:** pre-generated by `scripts/make_audio.py` into `learner/audio/`. Engines: `edge` (Microsoft's online neural voices, el-GR Athina and Nestoras, only with the learner's yes, content text only), `windows` (Stefanos, offline), `piper` (a low-quality CC0 Greek voice).
- **Whisper** (`faster-whisper`, model `small`) is speech-to-text for the learner's recordings. It does not make the voice and is not part of this phase.
- The maintainer's laptop has a 2 GB GPU (MX550): too small for large models, so heavy generation runs on the CPU, overnight if need be.

## The idea

The app knows its content in advance (words, chapter sentences, dialogues, listening passages, the films' captions). Speech does not have to be made live: it can be made once, slowly, with a heavy model, and kept as files the app plays. The browser voice stays as the fallback for anything without a file (a word the coach wrote today, before the next daily prep).

## Step 1 — a blind listening test (decide before building)

Take 10 sentences from the book (a word, a short sentence, a question, a dialogue line, a number, a name, a sentence with a hard stress) and make each with:

1. the current browser/Windows voice;
2. Microsoft's neural voice (edge-tts, el-GR-AthinaNeural and el-GR-NestorasNeural);
3. **Chatterbox Multilingual** (Resemble AI, MIT, Greek supported, about 500M parameters, runs locally; adds an inaudible watermark; can clone a voice from a short reference);
4. one Greek community model, for example **F5-TTS-Greek** (CC BY-NC 4.0) or a Greek Piper voice.

Check each model's licence and Greek support on its own page first, with the date. Publish a private page (an Artifact) with the clips shuffled and unnamed, so the maintainer can listen on the phone and rank them; name them after. Also note the time per sentence on this laptop's CPU.

## Step 2 — the pipeline

With the winner:

- `scripts/make_audio.py` (or a new `oknos voice`) makes a file for every vocab item (its `say` or `el`), every chapter sentence and dialogue line, and every listening passage, keyed by the text, so the same sentence is made once. Opus or MP3, small.
- The app plays the file when there is one, the browser voice when not. The tap-a-word reader, the learn card, the dialogue "play all" and the listening tasks all use it.
- `daily-prep` makes the audio for the coach's new items, so tomorrow's words are spoken well.
- Where the files live: the learner's machine (`learner/audio/` or `.cache/oknos/voice/`) unless the licence allows shipping them; a model with a non-commercial licence never ships audio in the open repo. Say which, per model.
- Local-first holds: a local model sends nothing anywhere. The edge engine stays opt-in and content-only (SPEC §9).

## Step 3 — voices for the story and the films

- The open book's people (`docs/brand/CHARACTERS.md`) can have their own voices if the model clones from a reference: Zeynep, Katerina, Giorgos, and a narrator. Reference clips must be original or openly licensed, never a real person's voice without their written yes.
- The films (`docs/brand/animations/`): each shot has a Greek caption; make one clip per shot (the narrator, or the character who speaks), set each shot's length to its clip, and add a "▶ Play with sound" button (browsers only play sound after a tap). Captions stay on screen. Optional: a quiet ambient bed (wind, cicadas) if it is original or CC0.

## Done when

The maintainer has picked a voice from the blind test; a fresh session's words, chapters and listening tasks play that voice from files, with the browser voice only as a fallback; daily prep keeps it up to date; and the three films play with narration.
